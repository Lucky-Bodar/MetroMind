⚙️Pipeline executed for every “Analyse trip” click

1 CollectTrip form (D2), sensor slot (D3), weather and road graph (D4).
2 PreprocessValidate inputs, snap places to the road graph, align to sections and 5-minute slots, drop personal details.
3 EstimateD1 baseline × weather, blended with live D3. Travel time = length ÷ speed, section by section.
4 DiagnoseRank sections by delay; the top one is the bottleneck, with its reason.
5 Guide & compareTest other departure times, an alternate route and other modes. A route is rejected if it pushes nearby roads above 75% congestion.
6 EvaluatePilot metrics: RMSE, MAE, R², ranking match, System Usability Scale.

Prototype note

This demo runs in the browser using approximate coordinates for 80 Bengaluru localities, a road graph built from the main corridors, sample values patterned on the D1 schema and a simulated live feed (the clock is real IST time; the sensor readings are simulated). Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap, CARTO and Esri when you are online, and routes are drawn as straight segments between the junctions of the road graph. Any other place can be added through online search (OpenStreetMap) when you are connected. All values are estimates; no traffic signal is controlled. Real accuracy figures will come from the one-month pilot.

❓Frequently Asked Questions & Transit Intelligence

What is MetroMind and how does it predict Bengaluru traffic bottlenecks?

MetroMind is an AI-powered spatio-temporal traffic optimization system tailored for Bengaluru. It models the city's 80 transit hubs and 17 primary corridors as a directed multigraph, continuously calculating section-level Travel Time Index (TTI) by fusing 65% real-time sensor streams (D3), 35% historical Kaggle machine learning baselines (D1), and meteorological weather coefficients (D4). It isolates the exact bottleneck edge where delay is maximized rather than simply providing a black-box total travel time.

How does MetroMind prevent neighborhood traffic jam spill-over (Braess's Paradox)?

Traditional navigation apps often greedily redirect vehicles into narrow residential streets when main highways slow down, worsening city-wide gridlock. MetroMind enforces a hard downstream capacity constraint: any alternate route that would elevate secondary collector road congestion above 75% is rejected, maintaining network equilibrium across neighboring communities.

Which major arterial corridors in Bengaluru are covered in the road graph?

MetroMind monitors 17 primary arterial corridors across Bengaluru, including Outer Ring Road (Silk Board to Marathahalli & Hebbal), Bellary Road (Kempegowda Airport corridor), Hosur Road (Electronic City Elevated Expressway), Whitefield Main Road, Old Madras Road (KR Puram), Old Airport Road, Bannerghatta Road, Tumkur Road (Yeshwanthpur/Peenya), and Kanakapura Road.

How is commuter privacy preserved during trip saving?

MetroMind implements strict differential privacy through section-level spatial quantization and a k-anonymity aggregation floor (k ≥ 5). No commuter names, telephone numbers, or continuous GPS breadcrumbs are stored. Trip records are persisted asynchronously with randomized identifiers (T-xxxx, U-xxxx) to an encrypted Neon Serverless PostgreSQL database only after explicit user consent.

How can commuters use the Corridor Dashboard for travel planning?

The Corridor Dashboard provides a 24-hour City Profile bar chart showing congestion intensity curves for weekdays and weekends (morning peak at 08:30 and evening peak at 18:30). Commuters and traffic operators can drag the real-time Corridor Ranking slider to view real-time corridor congestion hierarchy and check the anonymous D5 heatmap matrix.
